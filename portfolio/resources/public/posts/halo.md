10 years ago, smart homes were all the rage. The new Amazon Echo, Phillips Hue smart light bulbs, smart meters. The name smart popped up, with promise.

These devices were IoT or internet of Things. To the everyday person, this was a wonderful, mysterious placard for a device with all sorts of functionality. But are these devices really smart? Light bulbs could be triggered by commands remotely. Thermostats controlled by phones. You could speak to a helpful AI assistant and play music or order groceries. That said, the system didn't really think for itself. These parts were often disconnected, running through their own applications, and if they did talk to each other, it would be a single controller doing simple commands when asked.
